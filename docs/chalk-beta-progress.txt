    [x] 3.4 - Implement named versions, Trash retention, startup recovery, quota handling, and persistent-storage UX
        Evidence: a Coach marks the Play as it stands with a chosen label, and revision IDs are added rather than put so no automatic process can rename or overwrite a named version; a Play's history lists as metadata only.     [x] 3.5 - Add real IndexedDB migration fixtures and interrupted-write recovery tests
        Evidence: a fixture harness writes released records straight through IndexedDB rather than through the current repository, so the suite upgrades a database a previous release actually wrote instead of one today's code produced. That harness exposed a real durability defect: a stored Play at schema version 1 or 2 made both getPlay and loadPlaybook throw, so a Coach whose database predated the current schema could not open their Playbook at all. Reads now migrate a legacy Play forward, keep it in its own Playbook rather than the legacy-import bucket, and rehash it because the stored hash described the older shape; an explicit startup upgrade rewrites those Plays once, skips any a concurrent tab already moved, restores their search projections, and is a no-op on second run. A current Play whose hash no longer matches is still reported as corrupt rather than silently migrated. Interrupted-write coverage proves the Coach is left with either the prior valid state or the complete new state and never a mix: closing the device mid-commit rolls the Play, its revision, its sync mutation, and its undo history back together; a transaction that fails after writing leaves no history describing the rejected edit; losing every disposable derived record costs no Play; and a commit that was acknowledged before the tab died survives with its history and mutation intact.
    [x] 3.6 - Implement and verify encrypted backup export/import
        Evidence: a Coach's backup is encrypted on the device before it becomes a file, using PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600,000 iterations over a passphrase the Coach chose and AES-GCM with the header bound as additional authenticated data, so altering the recorded work factor or the ciphertext makes the file refuse to open. A wrong passphrase and a tampered file are reported identically, because both mean the same thing to the Coach. Every export uses a fresh salt and nonce, and no Play name, Playbook name, or Assignment text appears anywhere in the file. The payload carries the Coach's authoritative work and its immutable history — Playbooks, Concepts, Formations, Plays including their Trash marks, revisions, and preferences — while device-local queues, conflicts, undo history, the open-session marker, and derived previews stay behind. Backups are written strictly and read leniently: a file an earlier release wrote has its Plays upgraded on import and restored to the Playbook the Coach kept them in. Import validates everything before writing anything and writes in one transaction, so a rejected file leaves the device exactly as it was; merging never overwrites a Play the Coach edited after the backup was written and never rewrites an immutable version already stored, and replacing is the only way to discard current records. Round trips restore current work, Trash state, and named versions onto a new device with matching document hashes; the shell offers backup and restore with the passphrase the Coach chose, warns plainly that a lost passphrase cannot be recovered, refuses to send anything without one, and never uses replace mode.

Contextual actions closed 4.3 on 2026-08-09. Right-clicking a Player or a route, or holding a press on one, opens the original's five-item menu where the pointer is — Duplicate, Mirror, Bring forward, Send back, Delete — held clear of the far edges at the original's own margin so a menu asked for in the corner is still whole. Asking about something settles the selection the way the original settles it: what he already picked stays picked, so asking about one of several does not throw the rest away and Delete still takes the group, and anything else becomes the selection on its own. That rule is the machine's rather than the shell's, because it is a selection change arriving by a new modality and ADR 0016 puts every modality through one model; the shell's part is only where to draw the menu. Whatever was being drawn is abandoned, since the Coach has stopped to look. ADR 0016 also forbids a menu holding anything a keyboard cannot reach, which is what made Bring forward and Send back real rather than listed: they now walk a line through its layer one step at a time, skipping a neighbour that is also picked so a group keeps its own order as it travels, and walking the list from the back on the way forward so two picked lines cannot leapfrog. Players are not in it — they draw above every line whatever order they are stored in, which is why the original reorders only routes and text, and why on a Player both items are greyed from the same answer that would run them. How the reorder reaches the document is the domain's existing answer rather than a new one: a layer whose order changed is replaced wholesale rather than guessed at with a move script, so the step is expressed as an ordinary difference between the Play and the Play reordered, and the policy that was already written and tested is the one that runs. Fourteen machine tests mutation-checked against fourteen reintroduced defects, one of which showed a group test that could not tell the neighbour guard from its absence until the group was pushed against the back of its layer, where the only neighbour left is one of itself. Two defects of our own surfaced in the browser and neither was reachable from a unit test. The long press asked whether the press had become a drag before dispatching the move that makes it one, so a single decisive movement left the menu armed under a man already being dragged; it is asked after now. And the press hit-tested with mouse precision, which would have made this menu the one thing on the field a finger had to be accurate to open; it asks with the modality that pressed. One divergence recorded: the original allowed six pixels of tremor before giving up on the menu where production lets go at the two the machine already uses, because one press cannot both be dragging a man and offering a menu about him, and the machine is what decides which is happening.
