Corrected on 2026-08-08: the geometry seam was right, but the conversion feeding it was not. The original's canvas is anisotropic — 18.3 pixels per yard across the field against 12 downfield — and the legacy reader used the depth scale on both axes, so every crossfield distance it produced was half again too large. That is review finding #3, reproduced in the conversion rather than inherited from it, and it put the seeded Play's split end five yards out of bounds. Tests pin the conversion to the original's own 976-pixel field span, assert the drawn field fills its frame rather than only agreeing with itself, and hold every seed and golden inbounds; each was confirmed to fail against the defect it guards.

    [x] 3.2 - Implement transactional local commits and continuous save-state acknowledgement
        Evidence: the Chalk-owned vanilla Zustand EditorStore keeps title typing transient until blur or Enter, validates committed Play documents, serializes rapid saves, advances optimistic document-hash guards, creates durable sync mutations, and reports Saving on this device, Saved on this device, or Local save failed — retry without leaking React or Dexie through its interface. The measured interval includes queue wait plus IndexedDB acknowledgement. Unit tests cover draft/commit separation, ordered rapid edits, failure retention, retry, and budget classification; committing one Play into a 2,000-Play Playbook costs no more than committing into a two-Play Playbook, proving the commit path does not grow with Playbook size; and Chromium plus WebKit/iPad tests prove the Coach-visible acknowledgement, truthful budget classification, and close/reload durability through the production adapter. The strict 50 ms budget is asserted on developer machines and is guarded against gross regressions on shared CI runners, which are not target devices; item 4.6 owns proving it on the target devices.
    [x] 3.3 - Implement bounded persistent hash-guarded per-Play undo and redo
        Evidence: a Coach edit is now a semantic Play command — rename, notes, tags, Personnel Label, Play Type, unit, Concept and Formation source, Field Profile, Player insert/remove/move/update, route insert/remove/update, label and Assignment insert/remove/update, mirror, and one batch per gesture — carrying an inverse computed against the Play as it stood before the edit and producing a schema-validated Play. Deleting Players or routes cascades to dependent routes, bound labels, dangling Assignment actions, and Formation slot bindings as one undoable step, and clearing a layer is an ordinary hash-bound transaction. Per-Play history stores forward and inverse commands with expected before and after document hashes instead of Play snapshots, coalesces consecutive edits to one field until the Coach pauses, blurs, or moves to another field, clears only that Play's redo stack on a new edit, and retains at most 100 commands, 20 MiB of encoded history, or seven days. A step is offered only when the Play in front of the Coach hashes to the entry's expected boundary; anything stale, unreplayable, or hash-mismatched is quarantined instead of applied speculatively. History rides inside the Play commit transaction, stays device-local, and is discarded rather than trusted when it no longer parses. Command round trips, generated command-sequence properties, retention bounds, coalescing, redo clearing, quarantine, and restore-after-reopen pass without React or IndexedDB; real IndexedDB tests cover the transactional history write, stale-commit rollback, corrupt-history disposal, and a full 100-entry history committed at 2,000-Play scale without growing with Playbook size; and Chromium plus WebKit/iPad prove Coach-visible Undo and Redo through the production adapter across reloads.
    [x] 3.4 - Implement named versions, Trash retention, startup recovery, quota handling, and persistent-storage UX
        Evidence: a Coach marks the Play as it stands with a chosen label, and revision IDs are added rather than put so no automatic process can rename or overwrite a named version; a Play's history lists as metadata only. Restoring diffs the current Play against the version and applies the difference as ordinary commands, so a restore is one undoable entry rather than an out-of-band replacement, satisfying both the no-snapshots rule and the original's undoable restore. Deleting a Play hides it from the Playbook and drops its derived data while keeping the document, its versions, and its history recoverable for thirty days; a Play in the Trash cannot be committed to, and purging after retention is the only path that discards a Coach's Play. An open-session marker records that a session started and is cleared on a clean close, so the next startup can say the app closed unexpectedly without claiming work was lost. Chalk requests persistent storage, never asking twice once granted, grades quota pressure as healthy, watch, critical, or unknown, reports unknown rather than failing when the storage API is blocked, and offers to free the disposable previews and projections it can rebuild. The shell replaces the ambiguous Save button with an explicit Create version action and a save acknowledgement that is pressable only to retry a failure; Sync now stays deferred to Phase 7. Command-difference round trips and a generated property that any edit sequence reproduces its target, real IndexedDB tests for versions, Trash, purge, interrupted sessions, and storage grading, component tests for the version menu, recovery notice, storage pressure, and retry affordance, and Chromium plus WebKit/iPad proof that a named version outlives a reload and restores undoably all pass.
    [x] 3.5 - Add real IndexedDB migration fixtures and interrupted-write recovery tests
        Evidence: a fixture harness writes released records straight through IndexedDB rather than through the current repository, so the suite upgrades a database a previous release actually wrote instead of one today's code produced. That harness exposed a real durability defect: a stored Play at schema version 1 or 2 made both getPlay and loadPlaybook throw, so a Coach whose database predated the current schema could not open their Playbook at all. Reads now migrate a legacy Play forward, keep it in its own Playbook rather than the legacy-import bucket, and rehash it because the stored hash described the older shape; an explicit startup upgrade rewrites those Plays once, skips any a concurrent tab already moved, restores their search projections, and is a no-op on second run. A current Play whose hash no longer matches is still reported as corrupt rather than silently migrated. Interrupted-write coverage proves the Coach is left with either the prior valid state or the complete new state and never a mix: closing the device mid-commit rolls the Play, its revision, its sync mutation, and its undo history back together; a transaction that fails after writing leaves no history describing the rejected edit; losing every disposable derived record costs no Play; and a commit that was acknowledged before the tab died survives with its history and mutation intact.
    [x] 3.6 - Implement and verify encrypted backup export/import
        Evidence: a Coach's backup is encrypted on the device before it becomes a file, using PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600,000 iterations over a passphrase the Coach chose and AES-GCM with the header bound as additional authenticated data, so altering the recorded work factor or the ciphertext makes the file refuse to open. A wrong passphrase and a tampered file are reported identically, because both mean the same thing to the Coach. Every export uses a fresh salt and nonce, and no Play name, Playbook name, or Assignment text appears anywhere in the file. The payload carries the Coach's authoritative work and its immutable history — Playbooks, Concepts, Formations, Plays including their Trash marks, revisions, and preferences — while device-local queues, conflicts, undo history, the open-session marker, and derived previews stay behind. Backups are written strictly and read leniently: a file an earlier release wrote has its Plays upgraded on import and restored to the Playbook the Coach kept them in. Import validates everything before writing anything and writes in one transaction, so a rejected file leaves the device exactly as it was; merging never overwrites a Play the Coach edited after the backup was written and never rewrites an immutable version already stored, and replacing is the only way to discard current records. Round trips restore current work, Trash state, and named versions onto a new device with matching document hashes; the shell offers backup and restore with the passphrase the Coach chose, warns plainly that a lost passphrase cannot be recovered, refuses to send anything without one, and never uses replace mode.

    [ ] 4.3 - Implement every original player, route, branch, label, assignment, styling, snapping, and direct-manipulation behavior
        In progress — route creation landed on 2026-08-09. A Coach draws from the blue dot above a Player or with the Route, Motion, Block, and Zone tools: press to start on the man running it, press each break, and finish on Enter or a double click, committing one insert and one undo entry for the whole route rather than one per break. The drawing grammar is the original's, measured from the canonical source: breaks constrain to grass-true 45-degree increments while snapping is on and Shift frees them, a break landing within four pixels of the last is dropped, holding the pointer and pulling away past seven pixels bends that segment through the pointer by reflecting the control point across the chord's midpoint, and typed digits set a break's exact depth with Backspace trimming the number before it touches the route. Escape and Backspace step outward through the drawing — the typed depth, then the last break, then the route — before either reaches the Play, and switching tools abandons the route in progress. Routes clamp to the sidelines and the drawn frame, with rounding applied before clamping so a rounded value cannot cross back over the boundary it was just held inside. Each kind carries the original's defaults, and a second route on the same man arrives dotted as his alternate. Zone drops now classify by where they end and draw the original's default bubble until the Coach sizes them, with the classification thresholds converted on the correct axis. Thirteen machine tests and three render tests were proven non-vacuous by reintroducing seven defects; Chromium and WebKit/iPad draw a real route through the tool and through the blue dot, prove Escape leaves nothing behind, and prove the finished route undoes as one entry. Route editing followed on 2026-08-09: a selected route raises a handle on every break, a square in the middle of every segment, and the zone corner, all drawn at a constant screen size with hit areas that clear the 44 CSS pixel touch minimum. Dragging a break constrains it to 45 degrees from the break before it and then lets football landmarks claim it — the original's order, so an angle the Coach set is not undone by a landmark — and a curved segment keeps its bend because the control point travels with the break it belongs to. The curve handle rides the middle of its segment, bends it through the pointer, and straightens it again when dropped back within five pixels of the chord's midpoint, which is the only way back to a straight line once one is bent; the key is dropped rather than cleared so a straightened segment hashes identically to one never bent. The zone corner sizes the area a defender owns within the original's bounds, converted on the axis each belongs to, and reads out its width. Double-clicking a selected route adds a break to the segment the Coach pointed at. Each completed handle drag is one update and one undo entry, and a handle pressed without dragging only selects its break. Twelve more machine tests, mutation-checked against five reintroduced defects — one of which exposed a vacuous insert test that only ever exercised the first segment — plus Chromium and WebKit/iPad proof that a break drags and a segment bends through the real handles. Labels and the text tool followed on 2026-08-09, so a Coach can now finish a Play diagram without leaving production: the text tool writes a note where he pressed, hands the select tool back, and puts him straight into typing it. A note belongs to whichever unit he was working on rather than to where on the field it sits, because depth notes and progression numbers live downfield too. The Text panel replaces the idle inspector only while one note is selected — the state every parity golden captures is untouched, so the ratchets did not move — and offers the original's text field, Belongs to, Meaning, Size, Box, and colour swatches, where picking a meaning sets the whole look so a Landmark reads like every other Landmark on every card. The six meanings and their appearances now live in the domain beside the zone classification, being football semantics rather than shell styling. Retyping coalesces into one undo entry until the Coach leaves the field, and the input renders from a transient draft because binding it straight to the asynchronously committed text dropped keystrokes that landed mid-save. A note's leader line is a fourth handle kind, dragged to point at whatever the note is about. Two defects surfaced while wiring it, both invisible to unit tests and caught by driving the real browser. Selection was pruned of anything whose commit had not yet landed, so a Player, route, or note was deselected in the instant between the Coach making it and the save arriving; pruning now holds pending creations. And the field's default mousedown moved focus to the document body, tearing it back out of a note the Coach had just been put into typing; the field prevents that default, since it is driven by pointer events and needs none of it. Copy, paste, duplicate, and mirroring closed out the direct-manipulation verbs on 2026-08-09. The clipboard is transient editor state holding whole entities, so a paste survives edits to what it came from, or its deletion. Pasting makes new Players, routes, and notes rather than second references: a route follows its Player when both were copied, a note bound to a copied route rebinds to the copy, and one bound to a route left behind keeps its words and is placed by hand with the binding key dropped so it hashes like a note that was never bound. Copies land clear of their originals by the original's twenty-six canvas pixels, converted on the axis each belongs to. Duplicate is a copy and a paste in one press that neither reads nor disturbs whatever is on the clipboard. Mirroring with nothing picked is the domain's own reflection, which the double-mirror identity property already guards; mirroring a selection reflects only what the Coach picked, carrying each man's routes with him the way they follow when he is dragged, reflecting leader endpoints, and reflecting a bound note's offset rather than its position because the note rides its route. The Mirror action the More menu and command palette already listed is now real rather than shown unavailable. One divergence is recorded where it is made: the original detaches a route pasted without the man running it, which the production schema — requiring a Player on every path — has no way to express, so the copy stays attached to him instead. Eleven machine tests mutation-checked against five reintroduced defects, and Chromium and WebKit/iPad prove a paste is one undoable entry and that mirroring twice returns the Play exactly as it was.
